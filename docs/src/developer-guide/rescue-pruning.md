# Exact window pruning

Before a rescue job is staged, a filter decides from the exact K-mer matches between the mate and the window (5-mers at the default scoring) how much of the window the DP has to see. The code is `src/rescue_prune.h` (the lemma, the scalar filter and the dispatch), `src/rescue_prune_neon.h` (the NEON filter) and `src/rescue_prune_x86.h` (its x86 port); this page is the overview, and the headers hold the full arguments.

## The bound

Take the default scoring, match 1, mismatch -4, gap open 6 and extend 1 on both gap types, and a window and mate without N. A run of r matches holds max(0, r - 4) exact 5-mers and every mismatch costs 4, so a local alignment scores at most 4 plus the number of 5-mer hits on its cells, minus, for each gap, 2 plus the absolute change of diagonal the gap makes. Summing over the diagonals an alignment touches gives the bound the filter uses: an alignment whose diagonals span d1 to d2 scores at most 5 plus the sum, over the diagonals d from d1 to d2, of cnt(d) - 1, where cnt(d) is the number of 5-mer hits on diagonal d = i - j. The kernel's query-pad columns score at most +1, so they count as matches and the bound holds for them too.

Other scorings keep the argument with K-mer hits in place of 5-mers: a K for which the mismatch penalty, the deletion open alone and the insertion open plus extend each cost at least K - 1 matches (the insertion strictly more, so that the charge below is positive), a per-diagonal charge c set by the cheapest gap, the constant a (K - 1) + c in place of 5, and a tail scaled to the deletion costs (`rescue_prune_params`, whose comment in `src/rescue_prune.h` gives the formulas). Under `--meth` the hits come from one of two relations between read and reference bases. Converted copies: the exact matches of C-to-T (or G-to-A) converted copies of the window and the mate, which include every cell a bisulfite matrix scores as a match; the extra hits the conversion also makes (the mirror cell, reference T against read C) only loosen the bound. The exact relation (`rescue_prune_params::set_meth_rel`): a read T pairs with a reference T or C on the top strand (a read A with a reference A or G on the bottom one) and every other base only with itself, which is exactly the genomic matrix's match cells and a superset of the neutral matrix's (whose freed cell scores 0). The relation does not relate the mirror cell, which on mostly unconverted reads (TAPS) is what makes nearly every window a candidate under converted copies. It does not hold for the collapsed matrix, which scores the mirror cell as a match too, so `rescue_meth_rel` checks the run's two matrices themselves (`rescue_meth_rel_matrix_ok`: at most `a` on the diagonal and the freed cell, at most `-b` everywhere else) rather than inferring it from `--meth-scoring`. Gates: `rescue prune: the relation admits the genomic and neutral matrices, not collapsed`, `rescue prune: decisions at other scorings reproduce every consumed ksw_align2 field`, `rescue prune: --meth decisions reproduce ksw_align2 with the meth matrix`, `rescue prune: --meth relation decisions reproduce ksw_align2 with the genomic and neutral matrices`.

A maximum-sum interval over those per-diagonal terms is a Kadane scan, so the whole decision costs a pass over the hits and two scans over the diagonals.

## The decisions

`rescue_prune_window` returns one of three kinds:

- `RESCUE_PRUNE_B1`: no diagonal interval reaches the rescue threshold (`min_seed_len * a`), so the job's score is proven to fall short and none of its outputs can be consumed. `mem_matesw_batch_pre` does not enqueue it; its result slot holds `MATESW_GAR_PROVEN_FAIL`, which `mem_matesw_batch_post` treats as the ordinary failing rescue.
- `RESCUE_PRUNE_B2`: the rows from hb to he hold every row whose maximum can reach the threshold. The job is staged as that hull only, like the `--rescue-kmer` narrowing, and the post step adds hb back to the result's rows. A zero-state DP started at hb reproduces every field the rescue consumes: score, query and target ends, start positions and the suboptimal score. The header derives the tail slack that keeps the suboptimal score's row inside the hull. Gates: `rescue prune: B1 and B2 decisions reproduce every consumed ksw_align2 field`, `rescue_prune_identity.sh`.
- `RESCUE_PRUNE_FULL`: nothing is proven, and the full window runs as before. This covers an N in either sequence (it breaks the 2-bit K-mer code), lengths below 5 (below K on the filters themselves) or past the scratch capacity, a threshold a hit-free window reaches (at most (K - 1) a, so below 5 at the default scoring: `rescue_prune_params::from` refuses it), and a window sharing more hits with the mate than the hit gate allows. Gate: `rescue prune: N, a threshold below 5 and oversized inputs keep the full window`.

The hit gate, `BWA3_RESCUE_PRUNE_MAX_HITS` (default from `rescue_prune_max_hits`), exists because a dense window costs the filter more than the rows it saves. It changes only whether a job is filtered, never a decision's correctness, so every value of it leaves output identical. Gates: `rescue prune: B1 and B2 decisions reproduce every consumed ksw_align2 field`, `rescue prune: the SIMD filter's hit gate and query cache match the scalar filter`, `Banded rescue == kswv (rescue_band_harness, generated jobs)`.

## Where it runs

These checks in `src/bwamem_pair.cpp` and `src/rescue_prune.h` scope the filter, each on top of the last:

- `rescue_prune_on`: the build and `BWA3_RESCUE_PRUNE`. The filter runs only where a SIMD filter carries it (`RESCUE_PRUNE_HAVE_SIMD`: aarch64, and the x86 AVX2 and AVX-512BW builds); the scalar filter alone costs more than it saves against the kswv kernels.
- `rescue_prune_params::from`: the scoring. The lemma holds for any scoring whose mismatch penalty `b`, deletion open `o_del` alone and insertion open plus extend `o_ins + e_ins` each cost at least `(K - 1) a` for some `K` from 3 to 8 (`rescue_prune_scratch_kmax`), with a positive per-diagonal charge `c`; it takes the largest such `K` up to the caller's cap, which production sets with `rescue_prune_kmax` (8 wherever a SIMD filter runs, 5 under `--meth`, `BWA3_RESCUE_PRUNE_KMAX`), never below 5, and refuses the rest (`rescue_prune_params::valid` false), along with a threshold a window without hits could reach and any scoring kswv's 8-bit kernels do not run exactly (`kswv8_scoring_ok`: among others, each gap type's open plus extend must fit the byte kswv loads it into). `rescue_prune_runs` requires it. Under `--meth` (`rescue_prune_params::set_meth`) the filter matches C to T (OT) or G to A (OB) converted copies of the window and the mate exactly: every cell a meth matrix scores as a match becomes a hit, and the extra hits the conversion also creates only loosen the bound. The default collapsed scoring (`-B 2` against `-A 1`) is refused there; `--meth -B 4` and the genomic and neutral scorings are not. With TAPS chemistry at the genomic and neutral scorings the filter takes the exact relation instead (`rescue_meth_rel`, `BWA3_RESCUE_PRUNE_REL`). Gates: `rescue prune: scorings past kswv's 8-bit gap byte are refused`, `rescue prune: --meth decisions reproduce ksw_align2 with the meth matrix`, `rescue prune: --meth relation decisions reproduce ksw_align2 with the genomic and neutral matrices`, `rescue prune: K-mer decisions up to K = 8 reproduce ksw_align2 and the scalar filter`.
- `rescue_prune_runs`: the cost gate (`rescue_prune_cost_ok`). By policy, `--meth` prunes on aarch64 only: with EM-seq chemistry on converted copies, and with TAPS chemistry under the exact relation, since TAPS reads are mostly unconverted and their three-letter copies leave little to prune; the gate keeps `--meth` pruning off on x86 under either, where the measured `--meth` runs gained nothing (an EM-seq panel of 5 M pairs and TAPS, on an x86_64 host at the AVX-512BW tier; figures in the `rescue_prune_cost_ok` comment). The SIMD filters run at any threshold `rescue_prune_params::from` admits (`rescue_prune_params::valid`), so pruning runs at any seed length with a valid threshold, except on x86 at the AVX-512BW kswv tier from a seed length of 25 (`-k 25` and up, at any `-A`: scaling `-A` scales every score and the threshold alike): there the gate keeps pruning off because, in the runs it is based on (wgs-5M and wes-5M, prune on vs off at `-k 25`, 28, 32 and 40, on an x86_64 host at the AVX-512BW tier), the 64-lane kswv was cheap and few rescues passed at a long seed length, so the filter cost more than it saved. The SIMD filters take any `K = 5` bound weights with a match score of at most 16 (`rescue_prune_params::simd_ok`, `rescue_prune_params::simd_wt`), and `K` from 6 to 8 with exact matching; elsewhere the scalar filter decides, and the gate admits that only on aarch64, where it measured a gain (wgs-5M and wes-5M at `-B 6`, `-O 8 -E 2` and `-x intractg`, prune on vs off, on an aarch64 host at the NEON tier), so x86 prunes only where `simd_ok` holds: there the scalar filter lost (wgs-5M and wes-5M at `-O 8 -E 2` and `-x intractg` on the same x86_64 AVX-512BW host). Figures are in the `rescue_prune_cost_ok` comment. It also keys the length sort of the rescue jobs (`matesw_sort_partitions_by_len`), which groups narrowed windows so a SIMD lane group is not held to the length of one full window, (under `--meth` per OT / OB group), and the recording and reading of the hull offset. A run where it is false takes the pre-pruning rescue path, except that banded pass 1 still runs wherever `rescue_band_runs` holds: at the AVX-512BW tier at such a seed length, pass 1 is banded while pass 0 runs every window in full. Gate: `rescue_prune_identity.sh`, whose two legs at -k 25 run that combination on an AVX-512BW host and pruning at another threshold elsewhere.
- `rescue_prune_applies`: the job. Jobs on both kswv widths are filtered, but not under `--rescue-kmer`, which narrows windows its own way. The filter bounds scores, not SIMD lanes, and its hull counts the 8-bit kernels' query-pad columns (`kswv_query_quantum8`), never fewer than the 16-bit kernels pad (`kswv_query_quantum16`), so a 16-bit job's B1 and B2 decisions are exact too; only banding is 8-bit. Under `--meth` the job's hypothesis picks the conversion or the relation's strand. Gates: `rescue_prune_identity.sh`, `Banded rescue == kswv (rescue_band_harness, generated jobs)`.

Banded pass 1 has run gates of its own, separate from pruning's cost gate: the shortcuts on (`rescue_prune_on`), the banding path (`rescue_band_enabled`) and a scoring the band kernels take (`rb_scoring::valid`), for every one of which its band is derived, including scorings the lemma refuses. Without `--meth`, `rescue_band_runs` admits it in the symmetric-scoring batch. Under `--meth`, `rescue_band_meth_on` bands per OT / OB group with that group's matrix, by default (`BWA3_RESCUE_BAND_METH=1`) where `--meth` pruning is off (or under `--rescue-kmer`, which prunes nothing) or filters under the relation (TAPS), and then pass 0 too, which bands pruned parents and so by default runs only in the relation case (at `BWA3_RESCUE_BAND_METH=2` or more it bands always, converted-copy pruned parents included, and at 0 never); on top of converted-copy pruning (EM-seq) the band costs more than it saves (an EM-seq panel of 5 M pairs at the genomic scoring and `-B 4`, `-t 16`, band vs no band, on an aarch64 host at the NEON tier; figures in the `rescue_band_meth_on` comment). Within an admitted run, pass 1 bands an 8-bit job only when its band is cheaper than the full pass, and under `--meth` only when its prefixes hold no N, which the group matrix has no entry for (`RescueBandBatch::take_pass1`, `BWA3_RESCUE_BAND_P1`, `BWA3_RESCUE_BAND_P1_COST`). Gates: `rescue_prune_identity.sh`, `Banded rescue == kswv (rescue_band_harness, generated jobs)`.

## The NEON filter

`rescue_prune_neon::lean_neon` computes the decisions of the int16 reference `rescue_prune_neon::lean` and of the int32 `rescue_prune_window_scalar`, at any threshold (a run-time argument, `rescue_prune_neon::MINSC` being only its default). The numbered design notes above `lean_neon` in `src/rescue_prune_neon.h` walk through it: the hit rows found 16 at a time with a presence bitmap, repeated query 5-mers collapsed into runs by occurrence layer, the Kadane scans done 8 segments at a time in transposed registers, and the components read off two bitsets built in the backward scan. It takes the scoring's bound weights (`rescue_prune_neon::Wt`), with the default scoring's code kept as its own instantiation; a diagonal's hit test compares the prefix step against the charge, which a test for a non-negative step would get wrong on a single-hit diagonal once the charge exceeds the match score. Under the relation (the `Rel` instantiation) only the query side differs: the query table holds one entry per reference code a query 5-mer relates to (its code with any subset of its relx positions flipped), chained per code (`rescue_prune_neon::filter_rel_table`), so the hit gate and the hit counts stay exact counts, and the repeated-code layers walk entry ids instead of query positions (`rescue_prune_neon::filter_ent4`); a mate needing more than `rescue_prune_neon::REL_ECAP` entries keeps the full window, as on the scalar path. It works in int16, which is exact up to `rescue_prune_neon::HIT_CAP` hits (with the per-segment constants of the backward scan moved out of its loop) and, at other weights, while the weighted hit total and the charge over the diagonals stay within int16; a job past that, which only an opened hit gate lets through, or past the scratch capacity, returns `rescue_prune_neon::FALLBACK` and the scalar filter decides it. Gates: `rescue prune: the SIMD filter decides exactly as the scalar filters`, `rescue prune: the SIMD filter's hit gate and query cache match the scalar filter`, `SIMD rescue filter == scalar filter (rescue_prune_eq, generated jobs)`.

Longer K-mers (`K` from 6 to 8, the `KK` instantiations of `lean_neon_core` and `rescue_prune_x86::lean_x86_core`, exact matching only) change only steps 2 and 3. The query table is built over K-mers (`rescue_prune_neon::filter_kmer_table`: a table and presence bitmap over 4^K codes, allocated once and reset through the codes the previous query set), and step 3's vector test, still on 5-mers, becomes a prefilter: a row whose K-mer occurs in the query has its own 5-mer there too, and so do the `K - 5` rows before it, whose 5-mers lie inside that K-mer, so the row's presence bit is ANDed with theirs (the query table marks every query 5-mer for this). Each surviving row then gets its K-mer code and is kept if the K-mer bitmap has it and the row ends a K-mer (on x86 the codes are built with vector shifts and the bitmap is read with an AVX2 gather, 8 rows at a time). The layers, scans and components run unchanged on the K-mer table, with a hit's first row `K - 1` before its end. At any `K`, the hit total the gate computes also bounds the best interval (base plus a times the hits, minus c), so a job whose total cannot reach the threshold returns B1 before the layers and scans, the verdict the scalar filter reaches from the same sum. The relation's entry table is 5-mer only, so a relation job at `K > 5` goes to the scalar filter, and production never makes one. The scalar filter's code tables span 4^8 codes for the same reason and reset only the codes the previous query touched. Gates: `rescue prune: K-mer decisions up to K = 8 reproduce ksw_align2 and the scalar filter`, `SIMD rescue filter == scalar filter (rescue_prune_eq, generated jobs)`.

Two caches keep repeated work out of it (and out of the x86 port below), and both are exact because the cached result is a function of its key alone (Gates: `rescue prune: the SIMD filter's hit gate and query cache match the scalar filter`, `rescue prune: the SIMD filter's repeat memo and component list match a rescan`):

- The query table (codes, presence bitmap, occurrence chains) depends only on the oriented mate and the relation, and the mate repeats across every anchor rescued with it, so it is rebuilt only when the mate's bytes or the relation change.
- A job repeating the previous one byte for byte (same window, mate, lengths, hit gate, threshold, bound weights and relation) returns the previous decision without recomputing it; the per-diagonal arrays behind it were not touched in between, since only the filter writes them. Gates: `rescue prune: the SIMD filter's repeat memo and component list match a rescan`, `SIMD rescue filter == scalar filter (rescue_prune_eq, generated jobs)`.

One more cache sits outside the filter and rests on a byte comparison rather than on its key. The filter also keys its inputs (`rescue_prune_view` `keyed` / `key`: the window's first 32 and last 16 bytes and length, every mate byte, the hit gate, the threshold, the bound weights and the relation), and `mem_matesw_batch_pre` uses the key to find a repeat among the thread's last eight filtered jobs (`BWA3_RESCUE_REPEAT`): a job with a decision other than B1 whose inputs match one of them is answered from that job's kswv result instead of being enqueued again, since the kernel is a function of the staged bytes, the lengths and the score gate alone. Such repeats are the same mate rescued against identical windows, which anchors in identical repeat copies produce; about half of them repeat the previous job, most of the rest one of the seven before it. Before a regid is reused, the candidate's staged pair is compared with this job's inputs byte for byte (its window, mate, lengths, score gate, methylation hypothesis and hull offset), so the reuse is exact by that comparison whatever the key's history, a collision included; `mem_matesw_batch_post` reads the result by regid and applies the hull offset recorded for it, which equals this job's. Gate: `rescue_prune_identity.sh`.

## The x86 filter

`rescue_prune_x86::lean_x86` is a port of `lean_neon` to 128-bit SSE4.1 and SSSE3, which every AVX2 host has, so `rescue_prune_window` dispatches to it on the AVX2 and AVX-512 builds (`RESCUE_PRUNE_HAVE_SIMD`). It keeps the NEON data layout, scans, int16 capacity, both caches, the input keys and the relation's entry tables (the same helpers), so it returns the same decisions and leaves the same view. Two steps differ in how, not in what: the 128-byte presence-bitmap lookup is eight 16-byte table lookups joined by a blend tree on the index bits, and the two bitsets are built after the backward scan from the diagonal-major bound and hit counts. Gates: `rescue prune: the SIMD filter decides exactly as the scalar filters`, `rescue prune: the SIMD filter's hit gate and query cache match the scalar filter`, `rescue prune: the SIMD filter's repeat memo and component list match a rescan`, `SIMD rescue filter == scalar filter (rescue_prune_eq, generated jobs)`.

## The view

A B2 decision also returns a `rescue_prune_view`: the per-diagonal arrays it came from (hit counts, first hit rows, and either the bound itself from a SIMD filter or the two Kadane scans on the scalar path), plus, from a SIMD filter, the two bitsets and the filter's own list of components at the call's threshold (`rescue_prune_neon::Comp`, the first `NeonScratch::COMP_CAP` (or `X86Scratch::COMP_CAP`) of them stored and `ncomp` counting all). Band planning reads the view to find the diagonal components at any threshold, and takes the component list directly when it needs the whole window at that threshold, which is the common case. The view aliases the filter's per-thread scratch, so it is valid only until the next filter call on that thread; `mem_matesw_batch_pre` plans the job's bands straight after the call for that reason. Gates: `rescue prune: the SIMD filter's repeat memo and component list match a rescan`, `Banded rescue == kswv (rescue_band_harness, generated jobs)`.
